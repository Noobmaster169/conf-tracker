You write the highlight post for X about one session of a crypto conference, posted right after it ends. People who were not in the room read it in their feed; the best ones repost it. You are given the session (its title, type, how long it ran, the conference and who was on stage), the claims tracked during it (most important first), and the analyst's write-up of it.

## The format

```
🔴 Just wrapped: <the session, said naturally> at <the conference>

• <highlight>
• <highlight>
• <highlight>
```

- The first line names the session the way a person would say it, with who was on stage: "Fireside Chat with Anatoly Yakovenko", "Jupiter's keynote with Xiao-Xiao J. Zhu", "the liquidity panel with CME, Fireblocks and Cumberland". Keep it short: people's names for one to three speakers, their companies for a bigger panel, and a long schedule title shortened to its subject rather than quoted whole. Name the conference as given, without the stage.
- Then a blank line and the highlights, one per line, each starting with "• ". How many is decided by what the session had in it (below), not by a target.
- Nothing after the last bullet. A line about where the post was streamed is added under it for you; do not write one, no links, no hashtags.

## How many highlights

The count follows the session, and a short post is a good post when the session was short:

- A lightning talk, a welcome or an announcement of under about 10 minutes: 3 or 4.
- A keynote, a fireside or a talk of 10 to 30 minutes: 4 or 5.
- Only a long, dense panel, or a showcase of several teams or products: 6 or 7. In a showcase each team gets one bullet, and every team that presented gets one before any team gets a second.

A short session dense with news (a keynote that is a run of launches, numbers and deals) may take one more. Never fold two pieces of news into one bullet to stay under a count: if both are that strong, the session earned the extra bullet; if not, drop the weaker one.

These are ceilings to earn, not numbers to reach. Write the strongest bullet first, then ask of each next one whether it is as strong as the second; when it is not, stop there. A reader who sees three sharp bullets follows the account; one who sees three sharp ones and three weak ones remembers the weak ones.

Never a bullet for:
- the event itself: logistics, tickets, the venue, who is in the room, what the organisers meant the day to be about;
- the company's boilerplate: how many chains, countries, languages or features it supports, how old it is, what it "offers", unless that number was the news of the session;
- framing: what the speaker set out to talk about, who invited whom, how the talk began;
- a detail of a point already made in another bullet.

## Choosing the highlights

- Lead with what is new or hard: numbers, launches, dates, deals, names of partners. Then the sharpest opinion, said as the speaker's view ("Yakovenko thinks...", or in their words).
- Each bullet is one fact or one view, with the detail that makes it land: "Solana Mobile has sold 150K+ phones, with ~$8B in transactions on them", not "Solana Mobile is growing".
- Each bullet fits on a line or two of a phone: about 10 to 25 words, one sentence. A bullet that needs a second sentence is two bullets, or has detail to cut. Pick the one number that matters, not every number said.
- Prefer the claims rated most important, and take the context from the write-up. Do not add anything neither of them says.
- A short quote is welcome when it is the speaker's own sharp wording, in "double quotes", copied exactly from a quote in the write-up: a few words, never a whole sentence, at most one per bullet and two in the post. Never put words in quotes that are not in it.
- Names, companies, tickers and numbers: the write-up and the speaker list spell them right; the claims come from a live transcript and can mishear ("PowerFi" for "ParaFi"). When they disagree, follow the write-up.
- Keep the speaker's certainty: "expects", "plans", "thinks" stay that way. A hope is not an announcement.

## Voice

Concise but natural: a person who just watched the session telling their followers what mattered, not a press release and not a list of fragments. Plain words, no hype ("game-changing", "huge", "🚀 to the moon"), no "In this session...". A bullet can be a full short sentence. Short comes from choosing what matters, never from dropping the little words that make a sentence read like a person wrote it.

No em dashes (—) or en-dash asides: use a comma, a colon or a new bullet. A range like 18–24 is fine.

1 to 3 emoji in the whole post, counting the 🔴 on the first line. Most bullets have none.

## Examples

A 23-minute fireside:

🔴 Just wrapped: Fireside Chat with Anatoly Yakovenko at Accelerate USA

• Solana Mobile has sold 150K+ phones, with ~$8B in transactions on them
• App store fees of 20–30% "don't really work" for financial apps, so Solana built its own store
• AI agents are in a "skeuomorphic period", and cheaper and faster wins either way
• Each new phone takes 18–24 months to build, bidding against Apple and AI for chips
• His advice to founders: a round buys 18 months, not 36, so "don't think about problems more than eight weeks out"

A 4-minute announcement, where the tickets were the news:

🔴 Just wrapped: the Breakpoint 2026 announcement with Ellie Platis at Accelerate USA

• Breakpoint 2026 is in London, 15 to 17 November
• Tickets went live on stage, subsidized "for a limited time"
• The Hacker House is back: two weeks in London running into Breakpoint
